1 Transport. One transport for the whole studio. The large readout is the position you are hearing (bar · beat · tick, then time). Space plays and stops; Stop is fast, Play starts about a tenth of a second later because the stretch engine must prime.

2 Ruler. Click or drag to cue. The dashed cue line moves immediately; the solid playhead follows when the sound does. The loop band is drawn here and edited by dragging its ends.

3 Lane headers. Eight lanes in two banks: copper A (tracks 1–4) and aqua B (tracks 5–8), labelled with the stem role and bank letter as well as colour. Each header carries Mute, Solo and a peak meter; full faders stay on the Performance desk.

4 Clips. Region boxes in the bank colour, with cached waveform peaks of the real audio (synthetic here). Fade ramps darken the clip edges. The selected clip has the focus ring and two trim handles with 44 px hit zones.

5 Proposal. A Producer suggestion draws as a dashed, gold, unfilled clip labelled "Proposed". It is never mixed until approved, so committed music always looks solid.

6 Inspector. Opens when a clip is selected. Musical values first; "Exact values" reveals frames, source seconds and stretch rate for engineers.

7 Producer. Stays in the right column under the inspector, as on the Performance desk. Approve applies through the same validated edit path as a hand edit.

8 Playhead. Drawn from the audio clock at the moment the sound leaves the device, not from a timer. It never runs ahead of what you hear.
